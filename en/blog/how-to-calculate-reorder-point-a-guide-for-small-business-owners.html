How to Calculate Reorder Point: A Guide for Small Business Owners

Effective inventory management is the backbone of any successful small business. One of the most critical metrics you need to master is the Reorder Point (ROP). If you order too much, you tie up your cash in dead stock; if you order too little, you face stockouts and lose customers. This guide will walk you through the reorder point formula and how to implement it in your business.

Understanding the Reorder Point Formula

The reorder point is the specific level of inventory which triggers an action to replenish that particular inventory stock. The standard formula is:

Reorder Point = (Average Daily Usage × Average Lead Time) + Safety Stock

To use this, you need to understand three variables:

  1. Average Daily Usage: The average number of units sold per day.
  2. Average Lead Time: The number of days it takes for your supplier to deliver the goods after you place an order.
  3. Safety Stock: The buffer stock you keep to protect against spikes in demand or supplier delays.

Calculating Your Variables

To calculate your Average Daily Usage, look at your sales data over the last 90 days. Divide the total units sold by 90. If you sell 500 units of a product in 90 days, your daily usage is approximately 5.5 units.

Lead time is often overlooked. If your supplier takes 10 days to ship your order, your lead time is 10 days. Always account for communication delays or shipping hiccups. If you are unsure, add a 2-3 day buffer to your lead time.

The Role of Safety Stock

Safety stock is your insurance policy. If your demand is volatile, you need more safety stock. The formula for safety stock is: (Maximum Daily Usage × Maximum Lead Time) - (Average Daily Usage × Average Lead Time). Maintaining this buffer ensures that you never run out of products even during unexpected sales surges or shipping delays from your suppliers.

Implementing ROP in Your Small Business Toolkit

Once you have your ROP, you need to track it consistently. Manual tracking in a notebook is prone to error. Using a dedicated inventory spreadsheet is the most efficient way for small business owners to keep track of these levels. By automating the ROP calculation in your spreadsheet, you can receive alerts when it is time to reorder, allowing you to focus on growing your business rather than counting items on shelves. Consistent monitoring of these figures will improve your cash flow and ensure customer satisfaction by keeping your best-selling items always in stock.
