How to Calculate and Manage Inventory Obsolescence Cost for Small Business

Excess inventory that sits unsold for extended periods consumes your working capital and generates hidden costs that erode your profit margins. While 'carrying costs' cover the ongoing expense of storage, insurance, and handling, 'obsolescence cost' represents the total loss of value when inventory becomes unsellable. Distinguishing between these two is critical for maintaining a healthy cash flow.

Carrying Cost vs. Obsolescence: Understanding the Difference

It is vital to categorize your inventory costs correctly to make informed purchasing decisions:

| Cost Type | Definition | Financial Impact |

| :--- | :--- | :--- |

| Carrying Cost | Ongoing expense of holding stock (rent, utilities, insurance, labor). | Reduces net profit over time. |

| Obsolescence Cost | The total write-off of capital when an item loses all market value. | Immediate hit to balance sheet/working capital. |

If you hold 10,000 in inventory, your carrying cost might be 20% annually (2,000). However, if that inventory becomes obsolete, you face a 100% loss of the principal capital (10,000) minus any recovery value.

Calculating Obsolescence Risk: The Sales Velocity Method

To manage obsolescence, you must shift from tracking 'Months in Inventory' to 'Inventory Age vs. Sales Velocity'. If you have 500 units and sell 50 per month, your stock life is 10 months. If you have 500 units and sell only 5 per month, your stock life is 100 months—a clear obsolescence risk.

The Formula

Stok Ömrü (Months) = Total Units in Stock / Average Monthly Sales

Excel/Google Sheets Implementation

Set up your tracking sheet with these columns:

  • Column A: Item SKU
  • Column B: Units in Stock
  • Column C: Average Monthly Sales (Last 6 months)
  • Column D: Unit Cost ()
  • Column E: Stock Life (Months) -> `=B2/C2`
  • Column F: Obsolescence Value () -> `=B2*D2`

Real-World Case Study: Calculating the True Loss

Consider a shop holding 100 units of a fashion accessory that is no longer trending.

  • Total Cost: 100 units *10 = 1,000
  • Disposal Fee:200 (Cost to clear the warehouse space)
  • Salvage Value: 100 (Selling to a liquidator at 10% of cost)

Total Obsolescence Cost = (Cost of Unsold Stock + Disposal Fee) - Salvage Value

Total Obsolescence Cost = (1,000 + 200) -100 = 1,100

By identifying this risk early, you could have avoided the200 disposal fee and recovered more than $100 through proactive clearance.

Mitigating Obsolescence with Proactive Inventory Control

  1. Dynamic Reorder Points: Use your 'Stock Life' (Column E) to trigger automatic markdowns when an item's stock life exceeds 6 months.
  2. ABC Analysis: Classify items by value and volume. Focus your obsolescence monitoring on 'C' items that have low turnover but high holding volumes.
  3. Salvage Partnerships: Establish relationships with secondary market retailers before stock becomes completely obsolete.
