How to Calculate and Track Supplier On-Time Delivery Performance

For small businesses, supplier delays are not just a nuisance; they are a direct hit to your cash flow. Relying on gut feeling leads to excessive safety stock and lost sales. This guide provides an Excel system to quantify On-Time Delivery (OTD) and transform it into a decision-making tool.

The Financial Impact: Beyond Late Shipments

Every day a shipment is late, your Cash Conversion Cycle (CCC) extends. If a supplier promises a 14-day lead time but delivers in 20, you are forced to hold higher safety stock, which increases your 'Inventory Carrying Cost' (typically 20-30% of inventory value annually). By tracking OTD, you identify exactly how much working capital is tied up due to unreliable partners.

Setting Up Your OTD Tracking Spreadsheet

Create an Excel sheet with these columns:

  • A: Supplier Name
  • B: Order Date
  • C: Promised Delivery Date
  • D: Actual Delivery Date
  • E: Days Late (Formula: =MAX(0, D2-C2))
  • F: On-Time Status (Formula: =IF(E2=0, "Yes", "No"))

Use the MAX function to ensure early deliveries aren't penalized. For the 'On-Time Status', if your industry allows a 24-hour grace period, use: =IF(D2-C2 <= 1, "Yes", "No").

Building an OTD Dashboard with Pivot Tables

To visualize performance, highlight your data and insert a 'Pivot Table'.

  1. Drag 'Supplier Name' to 'Rows'.
  2. Drag 'On-Time Status' to 'Columns'.
  3. Drag 'Order Date' to 'Values' (set to 'Count').

This creates an instant breakdown of on-time vs. late orders per supplier. Use the 'Slicer' feature in Excel to filter by month or specific vendor to spot trends.

Advanced KPI Tracking: The COUNTIFS Method

To build an automated dashboard, create a summary table where cell G2 is the Supplier Name. Use this formula to calculate their specific OTD rate:

=COUNTIFS(A:A, G2, F:F, "Yes") / COUNTIF(A:A, G2)

This formula dynamically updates as you add new rows, allowing you to monitor suppliers in real-time.

From Data to Action: The Performance Review

Don't just store this data; use it to negotiate. When a supplier's OTD drops below 85%, send a 'Quarterly Performance Report'.

Sample Communication Template:

"Dear [Supplier], our records indicate an OTD rate of [X]% for Q3. This delay has increased our safety stock requirements and carrying costs by [Y]%. We require a corrective action plan to return to our 95% service level agreement (SLA) by [Date]."

By documenting these metrics, you shift from reactive firefighting to a data-driven supply chain strategy, ensuring your cash remains in your pocket rather than in stagnant, delayed inventory.
