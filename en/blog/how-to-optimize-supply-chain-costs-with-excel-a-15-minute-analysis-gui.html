How to Optimize Supply Chain Costs with Excel: A 15-Minute Analysis Guide

Supply chain costs are often the 'silent killers' of small business profit margins. If you aren't calculating your true landed costs and optimizing your order cycles, you are likely overpaying for inventory and tying up cash that could be used for growth. This guide shows you how to build a dynamic Excel system to control these costs in 15 minutes.

1. Calculating True Landed Cost with Variable Distribution

Simply adding unit price and shipping is a rookie mistake. You must account for customs, handling, and logistics fees. Use this table structure to calculate the true cost per unit:

| Item SKU | Unit Cost | Total Freight | Total Customs | Weight/Vol (kg/m3) | Unit Landed Cost |

| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |

| A-101 | 10.00 |200 | 50 | 50kg | = (B2 + (C2+D2)*(E2/Total_Weight)) |

Pro-Tip: Do not divide total shipping by unit count if items vary in size. Use a weight or volume-based distribution: `(Total_Freight_Cost * Item_Weight_Percentage)`. This ensures heavy or bulky items absorb their fair share of the logistics costs, preventing you from underpricing them.

2. The EOQ Calculation: Your Operational Data Table

To use the Economic Order Quantity (EOQ) model, you need accurate inputs. Create this 'Data Input' table in Excel to calculate your variables before running the formula:

| Variable | Description | Value Example |

| :--- | :--- | :--- |

| D (Annual Demand) | Total units sold per year | 2,400 |

| S (Order Cost) | Cost of one PO (Admin + Phone + Bank fees) |50 |

| H (Holding Cost) | Storage cost per unit per year | $4 |

Once your data table is ready, use the EOQ formula: `=SQRT((2DS)/H)`.

Using the example: `=SQRT((2240050)/4) = 245 units`. This is your 'sweet spot' order quantity that balances holding costs against ordering frequency.

3. Monitoring Procurement Variances

Cost spikes destroy margins. Set up a variance tracker to compare current supplier prices against your historical baseline. Use the formula: `=(Current_Price - Baseline_Price) / Baseline_Price`. If your variance exceeds 5%, it is time to renegotiate or audit your supplier’s recent invoice for hidden surcharges.
