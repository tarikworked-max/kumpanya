How to Calculate Days Sales of Inventory (DSI) to Improve Cash Flow

If your cash is tied up in unsold products, you are effectively paying to store your own capital. Days Sales of Inventory (DSI) is the critical metric that tells you exactly how long it takes for your inventory to turn into cash. In this guide, we will move beyond theory and calculate your specific DSI to identify bottlenecks in your supply chain.

Why DSI is the Pulse of Your Business

DSI measures the average time, in days, that a business takes to turn its inventory into sales. A high DSI indicates that you are holding too much stock or your products are not moving fast enough, both of which are silent killers of small business cash flow. By calculating this, you can determine if you have 'dead stock' that needs to be liquidated or if your reordering process is inefficient.

The DSI Formula: How to Calculate It

To calculate your DSI, you need two figures from your recent financial records: your Average Inventory and your Cost of Goods Sold (COGS). The formula is:

DSI = (Average Inventory / Cost of Goods Sold) * 365

  1. Identify your Average Inventory: (Beginning Inventory + Ending Inventory) / 2
  2. Determine your COGS for the period.
  3. Divide the Average Inventory by the COGS.
  4. Multiply the result by 365.

For example, if your average inventory is 20,000 and your COGS is100,000, your DSI is (20,000 / 100,000) * 365 = 73 days. This means your cash is locked in inventory for 73 days before it returns to your bank account.

Operational Steps to Lower Your DSI

Once you have your DSI number, take these three steps to improve it:

  • Analyze your 'slow-movers': Identify products with the highest holding time and offer a bundle deal to clear them.
  • Adjust your reorder points: Use the reorder point formula in our toolkit to ensure you aren't over-purchasing stock that sits on shelves.
  • Negotiate terms: If your DSI is high, talk to suppliers about smaller, more frequent deliveries to reduce your average inventory levels.
