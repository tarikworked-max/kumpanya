How to Calculate Reorder Point and Lead Time Demand for Small Business

Inventory management is not just about counting stock; it is about protecting your cash flow. If you order too early, you trap capital in idle inventory. If you order too late, you face the 'stockout trap'. This guide provides a robust, data-driven framework to calculate your Reorder Point (ROP) and secure your supply chain.

The Real Formula for Reorder Point

The ROP is your early warning system. To calculate it accurately, you must account for the variability in both demand and lead time. The professional formula is:

ROP = (Average Daily Usage × Average Lead Time) + Safety Stock

Calculating Safety Stock

'Unexpected demand' is not a vague concept; it is a measurable risk. Calculate your Safety Stock using this formula:

Safety Stock = (Maximum Daily Sales × Maximum Lead Time) - (Average Daily Sales × Average Lead Time)

Example: If your average sales are 10 units/day with a 7-day lead time, but in peak season you sell 15 units/day and lead time stretches to 10 days:

Safety Stock = (15 10) - (10 7) = 150 - 70 = 80 units. You need 80 units of buffer stock to survive volatility.

Moving Beyond Simple Averages

Using a simple average for 'Daily Usage' is dangerous if your business is seasonal. Instead, use a 30-day Moving Average.

  • In Excel, maintain a daily sales log.
  • Use the formula `=AVERAGE(B2:B31)` to calculate your demand based on the last 30 days of performance. This ensures your reorder point adjusts automatically as your business grows or slows down.

Automating Alerts with Conditional Formatting

Don't manually check your stock. Make Excel tell you when to act:

  1. Select your 'Current Stock' column cells.
  2. Go to Home > Conditional Formatting > Highlight Cells Rules > Less Than.
  3. In the box, type the cell reference of your 'Reorder Point' (e.g., =$E2).
  4. Select 'Light Red Fill with Dark Red Text'.
  5. Click OK. Now, any cell that turns red is your immediate signal to place an order.

Risk Management: Why Lead Times Fail

Lead times rarely stay constant. Prepare for:

  • Seasonal Logistics: Courier delays during holidays.
  • Supplier Capacity: If your supplier is also a small business, their own production bottlenecks will affect you.
  • Quality Issues: Always factor in a 'buffer' if your supplier has a history of defect rates.
