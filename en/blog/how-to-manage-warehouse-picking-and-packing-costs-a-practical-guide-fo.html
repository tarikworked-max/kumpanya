How to Manage Warehouse Picking and Packing Costs: A Practical Guide for Small Businesses

Inefficient warehouse operations can quietly drain your working capital. For small businesses, picking and packing items for shipment often accounts for up to 50% of total operating expenses within the warehouse. If you do not track and optimize these labor-heavy processes, your profit margins will shrink long before orders reach your customers.

In this guide, we will break down the exact formulas, operational steps, and Excel structures needed to audit, calculate, and minimize your picking and packing costs without sacrificing fulfillment speed.

Understanding Picking and Packing Cost Drivers

Before you can reduce costs, you must understand where the money goes. Fulfillment expenses are generally split into direct labor (the time employees spend walking, picking, and packing) and material costs (boxes, tape, dunnage).

To calculate your baseline cost per order, use this fundamental formula:

Cost Per Order = (Total Monthly Fulfillment Labor Cost + Total Packaging Material Cost) / Total Orders Shipped in Month

For example, if your monthly warehouse labor is 4,000, your boxes and tape cost1,000, and you ship 2,500 orders, your cost per order is (4,000 +1,000) / 2,500 = $2.00 per order.

Step-by-Step Excel Setup for Tracking Fulfillment Labor

To manage this in Excel or Google Sheets, set up a simple tracking sheet with the following columns:

  • Column A: Order ID
  • Column B: Picker ID
  • Column C: Pick Start Time
  • Column D: Pick End Time
  • Column E: Total Pick Time (Minutes)
  • Column F: Hourly Wage Rate
  • Column G: Labor Cost Per Order

In cell E2, calculate the elapsed picking time with the formula:

= (D2 - C2) * 1440

(Note: 1440 represents the number of minutes in a day, converting Excel's time serial number into total minutes).

In cell G2, calculate the direct labor cost using:

= (E2 / 60) * F2

Optimizing Warehouse Layout to Reduce Travel Time

Studies show that up to 50% of a picker's time is spent walking between aisles. To minimize this, use the ABC Inventory Analysis method:

  • A-Items (Top 20% best-sellers): Place these items closest to the packing stations and at waist height.
  • B-Items (Moderate movers): Place them in secondary aisles.
  • C-Items (Slow-moving goods): Place them on upper shelves or in the back of the warehouse.

By reducing the average walking distance per pick by just 30%, you can decrease your picking labor costs proportionally.

Streamlining Packaging Materials and Dimensional Weight

Another hidden profit killer is oversized packaging and excess void fill. Shipping carriers charge based on Dimensional (DIM) Weight, meaning you pay for the space the box takes up, not just its actual weight.

To track and optimize material usage in your spreadsheet, add a column for Box Size Category (Small, Medium, Large) and audit your packing stations weekly. Ensure that staff use the smallest possible box that safely fits the product.
