FIFO vs LIFO: Which Inventory Valuation Method Saves You More Cash?

Inventory valuation isn't just an accounting rule; it's a cash flow strategy. Choosing between First-In, First-Out (FIFO) and Last-In, First-Out (LIFO) directly impacts your taxable income and your bank balance.

Understanding the Core Logic

FIFO (First-In, First-Out) assumes the oldest items in your inventory are sold first. LIFO (Last-In, First-Out) assumes the most recently purchased items are sold first. In an inflationary environment, these two methods yield drastically different results for your Cost of Goods Sold (COGS) and ending inventory.

The Financial Impact: A Practical Comparison

Imagine you sell 100 units of a product in a single batch. You previously purchased 50 units at 10 each, and a subsequent 50 units at15 each due to supplier price hikes.

  • FIFO Calculation: You sell the oldest stock first. Therefore, your COGS consists of all 50 units at 10 plus all 50 units at15. Your total COGS is (50 × 10) + (50 × 15) =500 + \750 =1,250. Your ending inventory is zero.
  • LIFO Calculation: You sell the newest stock first. Your COGS consists of the 50 units at15 and the 50 units at 10, yielding the same total COGS of\1,250 if all stock is cleared. However, if you only sold 50 units total instead of 100, LIFO would drain the 50 units at 15 first (COGS =\750), leaving the cheaper 10inventory on your balance sheet, reducing your taxable profit today.

Critical Legal Warning: Is LIFO Legal Everywhere?

Before choosing LIFO, small business owners must check local tax and accounting regulations. LIFO is strictly prohibited under International Financial Reporting Standards (IFRS) and is not allowed in countries following IFRS (including Turkey's TMS/TFRS standards). Even in the United States, where the IRS permits LIFO, adopting it triggers the 'LIFO Conformity Rule', meaning if you use LIFO for tax purposes, you must also use it for your financial statements. Always consult a certified accountant regarding your local tax jurisdiction.

How to Implement FIFO/LIFO in Excel

A simple `=SUMPRODUCT` function cannot handle multi-layered FIFO or LIFO inventory draining because it doesn't track inventory chronological layers. To build a true FIFO tracker in Excel:

  1. Set up a transaction table with columns: `Date`, `Batch ID`, `Units Purchased`, `Unit Cost`, and `Units Remaining`.
  2. Create an outbound log where each sale deducts units starting from the oldest `Batch ID` row using an `IF` and cumulative sum lookup (`INDEX/MATCH`).
  3. For LIFO, sort your inventory purchase log by `Date` descending so that formulas pull the latest unit costs first.
